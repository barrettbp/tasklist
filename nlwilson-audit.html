UX · UI · SEO Audit

Good local foundations. The biggest gains now come from clarity and quality control.

NL Wilson already has the trust signals, service breadth and local-market history needed to convert. The bigger opportunity is to make the site feel intentionally structured instead of template-driven.

Focus: conversion UXFocus: IA & navigationFocus: local SEOReviewed Sep 2026

First impression

The site communicates legitimacy quickly, but inconsistency in page content and structure undermines the trust those signals create.

Trust foundation

Strong

Years in business, phone visibility, Bekins affiliation, regulatory information and geographic relevance are all useful conversion signals.

Information clarity

Mixed

Services are easy to discover, but navigation mixes services, resources and specialties in ways that make the hierarchy harder to understand.

Content QA

Risk

Template mismatches and city-name errors are more damaging than a lack of content because they directly weaken trust and local relevance.

The central finding

The site does not need “more SEO copy” first. It needs stronger information architecture, consistent conversion patterns and a systematic QA pass across service and location templates.

Storage content mismatchStorage pages contain copy describing long-distance moving, indicating template leakage between services.
Location-page mismatchSome city pages reference other cities, which creates immediate credibility and local SEO problems.
Typos in high-intent areasErrors such as “Shot-Term Storage” reduce polish precisely where prospects are deciding whether to trust the company.

Top 5 quick fixes that win big

These changes deliver the most leverage without requiring a complete redesign.

Run a site-wide content and template QA pass

Audit every indexable service and city page for wrong city names, wrong service descriptions, broken template variables, duplicated sections and title/H1 errors.

Critical

Rebuild the header around user intent

Separate Moving, Storage, Cleaning, Service Areas and Resources. Move educational content out of the Moving menu and expose Service Areas in primary navigation.

High impact

Standardize the primary conversion action

Use one sitewide primary CTA: “Get a Free Quote.” Pair it consistently with “Call (913) 357-5330.” Avoid multiple names for the same action.

Quick win

Turn Pricing into a decision-support page

Explicitly label hourly units, minimums and surcharges. Add “what’s included,” example calculations and a clear path to an exact quote.

High impact

Make Contact a real conversion landing page

Add a strong H1, form context, phone, hours, service area, trust signals and a simple “what happens next” sequence to reduce uncertainty.

Quick win

Sitewide UX / UI audit

The visual foundation is workable. The main experience issue is that repeated components do not consistently help users scan, compare or decide.

Navigation

Too many sibling choices under Moving

Nine items under one parent creates a flat menu. Group specialty moving separately and move Packing Tips / Checklist into Resources.

Labels

Use self-explanatory menu labels

“Residential,” “Apartment,” “Commercial” and “Gun Safe” should become “Residential Moving,” “Apartment & Condo Moving,” “Commercial & Office Moving,” and “Gun Safe Moving.”

Visual scanning

Repeated generic imagery makes cards blend together

When multiple service cards use the same truck illustration, users lose the visual cues that help them skim. Use service-specific imagery or icons.

Social proof

Testimonials should match page intent

Use a long-distance testimonial on Long Distance, a storage testimonial on Storage, and a cleaning testimonial on Cleaning instead of recycling the same few quotes everywhere.

Orientation

Add breadcrumbs to deep pages

Patterns like Home → Storage → Long-Term Storage improve orientation, internal linking and hierarchy signals for search engines.

Mobile conversion

Keep the two primary actions persistently accessible

On mobile, a sticky bottom bar for “Call” and “Get Free Quote” would reduce friction on high-intent service pages.

Header, sitemap & information architecture

The current structure exposes many pages, but the conceptual model is not as clear as the business itself. Reorganize around service category, geography and resources.

Recommended primary navigation

  • Moving Services
  • Storage
  • Cleaning
  • Service Areas
  • Pricing
  • Resources
  • About
  • Contact

Keep Get a Free Quote visually distinct from navigation.

Why Service Areas deserves a top-level home

NL Wilson already has multiple city landing pages, but geographic discovery is underrepresented in the primary navigation. A Service Areas hub improves user discovery, internal linking and the crawl path into local pages.

Sitemap UX

Keep /sitemap.xml for search engines, but if the footer says “Sitemap,” point humans to a real HTML sitemap that groups services, locations, resources and company pages.

SEO priorities

The highest-value SEO work is structural and quality-focused, not publishing volume.

PriorityWhat to doWhy it matters
1Fix incorrect / duplicated city and service content.Protects trust and strengthens page-level relevance.
2Normalize titles, H1s and template metadata.Removes avoidable crawl and snippet-quality problems.
3Improve navigation and internal linking.Helps both users and crawlers understand page relationships.
4Create a Service Areas hub and stronger location clusters.Makes local landing pages discoverable and contextually linked.
5Add / verify BreadcrumbList, LocalBusiness / Organization and relevant Service schema.Improves machine-readable entity and hierarchy signals.
6Strengthen existing location pages with genuinely local information.Reduces doorway-page/template risk and improves usefulness.
7Publish more editorial content only after the above.Prevents spreading effort across more URLs before core pages are strong.

Local SEO quality rule

For city landing pages, quality is more important than count. Add neighborhoods, local move scenarios, city-specific FAQs, real jobs / photos, nearby-area links and local testimonials before creating more city pages.

Page audit

Pricing

Potentially one of the strongest conversion pages because real rates are published. The opportunity is to make the numbers unambiguous.

Open live page ↗

Turn “price information” into “decision support.”

People do not just want numbers. They want to understand what the numbers apply to, what the minimum is, what gets added, and what a realistic move might cost.

Clarity

State the unit next to every rate

If $210 is hourly, write $210 / hour. Do not make visitors infer the billing unit from a separate three-hour-minimum note.

Structure

Name each pricing category

Use explicit headings such as “Moving With Truck,” “Labor-Only Moving,” and “Gun Safe Moving” instead of repeating generic Description / Starting Rate tables.

Transparency

Explain every surcharge

Clarify trip / fuel surcharges, distance rules, stair or specialty fees, packing materials and exactly when billed time starts.

Conversion

Show one worked example

Example: crew × hours + applicable surcharge = estimated local move. Then route to “Get an exact quote.”

Page audit

Contact

The highest-friction page in the audited set because it relies heavily on the form without doing enough to reduce uncertainty around the next step.

Open live page ↗
SEO / UX

Give the page a meaningful H1

Use something like “Get Your Free Moving Estimate” or “Contact NL Wilson Moving & Storage in Olathe.” The page should have a clear purpose even if the form script fails to render.

Trust

Put confidence signals beside the form

Phone, business hours, location, service area, licensing, Bekins affiliation and a brief review signal belong next to the conversion form.

Recommended page flow

Tell us about your move

Ask only for the details needed to qualify and respond efficiently.

We review the details

Set an expectation for response channel and typical next step.

You receive your estimate / next steps

Remove ambiguity about what submitting the form actually triggers.

Add a secondary path: “Looking for pricing first? See Moving Rates →” so visitors can self-educate without abandoning the funnel.
